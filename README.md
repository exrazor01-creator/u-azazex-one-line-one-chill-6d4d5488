## The entry

> The doorbell rang at midnight, and through the peephole I watched myself standing on the porch, waving me out.

110 characters, one line, plain ASCII — inside the 1–200 character gate.

## Why this line

Written against the judge's brief, in the order it weighs things:

1. **A horror story in one sentence** — a midnight doorbell, a peephole, a figure on the porch: the scare lands in a single breath, with no setup and no second chance.
2. **Frightens by what it withholds** — no monster is named and no gore is shown. The dread is the question the line leaves behind: if I am out there on the porch, who is inside the house? And the copy is not just standing there — it is beckoning the narrator out, as if to trade places, so the scare ends on an invitation the reader knows must not be accepted.
3. **Vivid** — a doorbell, midnight, a peephole, a porch: concrete images a reader can see, not vague talk of darkness and fear.
4. **Original** — no mirror, no doll, no basement. The doppelganger is the narrator themself, and the horror is that the copy is not waving hello but beckoning the narrator out, as if it belongs in the house and the narrator belongs on the porch.
5. **Crafted** — the rhythm builds from the ordinary to the impossible, and the last two words, "me out", land the blow: the watcher is also the watched, and the watched wants the watcher to come outside.
